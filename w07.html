13:20 – 14:10 · BLOCK 1

The claim, seed variance, baselines

slides 5–19 · key points 1 to 3

What we cover

  1. What you are actually claiming when you write 83.7 in a table, and the two independent things that can go wrong.
  2. Seed variance: the same code and the same data give a different answer every run. Real figures from a paper that measured it.
  3. How many seeds is enough, and why reporting your best run is not a comparison.
  4. Baselines: why 94% accuracy can mean your system learned nothing, and the three baselines to run.
  5. Ablation: how to find out which part did the work.

14:25 – 15:10 · BLOCK 2

The three splits, metrics, significance

slides 20–32 · key points 4 to 6 · break first

What we cover

  1. Train, dev and test, and what each one is for. What tuning on the test set buys you, in points.
  2. Why the test set is looked at once, and why the leak always flatters you.
  3. Metrics: accuracy against F1, micro against macro, and what counts as a correct span in HW1.
  4. The paired bootstrap, worked through on eight items, and why a t-test on two numbers is not a test.

15:10 – 15:45 · BLOCK 3

Published numbers, and the table you hand in

slides 33–40 · key points 7 to 8

What we cover

  1. Why you cannot compare your number to the one printed in a paper, and the six things that differ between them.

15:45 – 15:57 · BEING CALLED ON

Ninety seconds, by seat number

Everybody answered every quiz question correctly on paper, so I will not ask for answers. I will ask for reasoning. Be ready to talk through one of these:

  • Why does saying "we report the best of five runs" not repair the comparison?
  • Why can an ablation table with one run per row not attribute the gain to any component?
  • Why is a t-test on two accuracy numbers not a test?

What I am listening for: that you name the mechanism, use a number from today correctly, connect it to your own project, and say precisely what you are still unsure about. The last one is worth as much as the first.
